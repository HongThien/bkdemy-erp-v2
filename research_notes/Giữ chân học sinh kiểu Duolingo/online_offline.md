# Online + Offline (blended / "phygital" / O2O): how physical programs plus an app drive engagement and retention, with lessons for BK Academy

> Researcher notes, 01/10/2026. About 22 tool calls. Evidence grade is marked as **[RCT/meta]** (peer-reviewed experiment or meta-analysis), **[company]** (company filing or official page), **[secondary]** (blog or aggregator, unverified), or **[marketing]** (vendor or franchise self-claim). Several primary pages (Niantic/Scopely blog, Starbucks 2026 press release, pokemonblog) returned 404 or 403 and could not be read in full; those claims are search snippets only.

## Q1. Do tutoring and learning centers with apps exist, and what do the apps do between classes? Is there retention data?

### Takeaway
The "physical center + app" model is common, not unique. Kumon, Mathnasium, VUS, ILA, Korean hagwons and Chinese OMO players (TAL/Xueersi) all run it. But almost all of these apps are either **digital worksheets plus a parent-reporting channel** (Kumon, VUS, hagwon apps) or **an in-center physical reward economy without a game layer** (Mathnasium). I found no tutoring operator that publicly combines a student-facing game economy (ranks, quests, coins, social feed) with on-site game events the way BK does. BK's real differentiator is that **combination**, not "online + offline" by itself. No operator publishes app-specific retention data, and the TAL renewal figure predates China's 2021 crackdown.

### Cited Findings
- **Kumon Connect**: digital delivery of Kumon worksheets on a tablet with a stylus. Students see the previous day's graded worksheets and their homework in a web app. Instructors can "replay" a student's work and adjust the plan. Parents monitor progress in real time — [Kumon US: How Kumon Connect works](https://www.kumon.com/resources/how-kumon-connect-works/); [Kumon UK](https://www.kumon.co.uk/kumon-connect)
- Kumon North America officially rolled out Kumon Connect on 1 March 2024 after a pilot. "95% of parents" in the pilot said their child was "more engaged or as engaged" on a tablet. **[marketing]** This is parent self-report, and "as engaged" counts toward the 95%, so it is a weak claim — [IFA / Franchise.org 2024](https://www.franchise.org/2024/03/kumon-connect-brings-even-more-personalization-to-learning/)
- **Mathnasium** uses a physical reward economy. Each student has a card that is stamped for every error-free page, and stamps are also given for good test grades at school. Full cards are redeemed at an in-center "prize cabinet" (candy, toys, gift cards, and at some centers Nintendo Switch or iPhone). Some centers use **prize wheels, Plinko machines and mystery boxes**. **[marketing]** These are individual franchise pages with no outcome data — [Mathnasium Long Beach](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/longbeachlosaltos/news/rewarding-students-is-part-of-the-program); [Mathnasium Glendale FAQ](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/glendale/news/faq-why-do-we-offer-rewards-amp-incentives-at-mathnasium); [Mathnasium Paradise Valley](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/paradisevalley/news/rewards-mathnasium-paradise-valley)
- **VUS (Vietnam)**: the OVI Parents app lets parents track learning progress, attendance, test scores and schedules. **V-Stars** is a loyalty program where parents earn stars (including from referrals) and exchange them for scholarships and gifts for their children. The points economy is aimed at the **parent and referrals**, not at the student's study behavior — [App Store: OVI Parents](https://apps.apple.com/vn/app/ovi-parents/id6475616797); [Thegioididong: App VUS](https://www.thegioididong.com/game-app/app-vus-ung-dung-thong-tin-lien-lac-giua-phu-huynh-hoc-256700); [VUS referral terms](https://vus.edu.vn/post/dieu-khoan-quy-dinh-chuong-trinh-gioi-thieu-hoc-vien)
- **ILA** has an "ILA Smart Learning" app on the App Store. I could not read its feature details — [App Store: ILA Smart Learning](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ila-smart-learning/id6746847119)
- **Korean hagwons**: apps such as 학원친구 ("Hagwon Friends") push real-time notifications to parents on arrival/departure check-in, test scores, homework and tuition, with messaging to staff. 학원 다녀오겠습니다 offers free attendance and notice management. The core is **check-in → parent notification**, not student gamification — [Google Play: 학원친구](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=kr.co.aca2000.messenger&amp%3Bhl=en_US&amp%3Bgl=US); [Google Play: 학원 다녀오겠습니다](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=dev210202.goingtohakwon)
- **TAL/Xueersi** was an OMO (Online Merge Offline) pioneer. It ran dual-teacher classrooms (an online lead lecturer plus a local offline tutor). One source says renewal for online grades 1–5 courses was "over 90%", "level with offline". **[secondary]** The figure is dated, from before China's 2021 tutoring ban — [EdTech China / Medium](https://edtechchina.medium.com/chinas-online-k12-tutoring-may-spark-the-next-100-billion-company-amid-intense-advertising-war-e2f285adb5f7); [eLearn Magazine: OMO](https://www.elearnmagazine.com/outcomes/omo/)
- After the 2021 regulation, Chinese tutoring firms were forced to restructure their business models — [CGTN 2021](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2021-08-07/After-school-tutoring-firms-face-strategic-remodeling-amid-regulation-12wwWRCFkQw/index.html). A Baiguan analysis notes TAL's offline learning centers are expanding again in the "new era" — [Baiguan](https://www.baiguan.news/p/investment-opportunities-china-after-school-tutoring-edu-tal-new-era-policy-normalization-autumn-quarter-performance-growth-engines-ai-technology-bigone-data-gross-billings-segment-growth-learning-centers-offline-expansion-k-12-tutoring-grade-market-smart)

### Inferences
- The industry has two archetypes. (a) **App = the parent's information channel** (Kumon, VUS, hagwons). (b) **Physical reward economy in the center** (Mathnasium: stamps → prize cabinet, prize wheel). BK combines both and adds a third layer: a **student-to-student social game** (ranks, feed, game nights). The "nearly unique" claim holds for **that combination**, not for "having both online and offline".
- Mathnasium's prize wheel and prize cabinet are the closest precedent for BK's "check-in prize wheel + xu redeemed for real rewards". The concept is already proven commercially in tutoring, but there is no public effect data.
- Korean hagwons show that the **"check-in → instant parent notification"** feature is an industry standard, and parents expect it.

### Gaps
- I found no public retention or renewal data for any tutoring center app (Kumon, Mathnasium, Eye Level, VUS, ILA, Apax). Retention numbers from these operators are internal.
- Not researched for lack of budget: Gaotu, Zuoyebang, Eye Level, Byju's Tuition Centres (BTC), Singapore centers.
- I could not read ILA Smart Learning's features or find any data on how VUS V-Stars affects re-enrollment.

## Q2. Fitness and blended: how do in-person events feed app engagement, and vice versa?

### Takeaway
The clearest pattern is **Orangetheory**: an objective metric is **measured in the room** (heart rate → Splat Points), shown **on the in-room TV**, and then **follows the member into the app** (summary, history, challenges). Strava shows the reverse direction (online community → real-world meetups growing fast). Peloton is betting on more in-person events to retain users. All the retention numbers attributed to these brands come from secondary sources or are self-reported, not audited.

### Cited Findings
- **Orangetheory**: a "Splat Point" is one minute in the orange/red heart-rate zone, with a target of 12 or more per class. Because the zones are tied to each person's heart rate, the bar rises as fitness improves, so progress never stalls. Results are shown in class, then sent by email and app after each session, and the leaderboard creates peer pressure — [Kobie: Orangetheory gamification](https://kobie.com/orangetheory-driving-loyalty-through-gamification/); [Loyalty Reward Co](https://loyaltyrewardco.com/orange-theory-fitness/)
- Claim of an "average member lifespan of 18.4 months (2025)", about double a traditional gym, and Orange Nation challenges with ">60% participation". **[secondary, low quality]** This comes from a business-model template site and has no primary source — [businessmodelcanvastemplate](https://businessmodelcanvastemplate.com/blogs/competitors/orangetheory-fitness-competitive-landscape)
- Orangetheory publishes a data-driven year-end recap ("12 Days of Fitness") celebrating the year's most popular workouts. This is a "Year in Review" pattern that turns offline data into shareable content — [BusinessWire Nov 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241125821512/en/Orangetheory-Fitness-Unveils-12-Days-of-Fitness-A-Data-Driven-Celebration-of-2024s-Most-Popular-Workouts)
- **Strava (2025 Year in Sport)**: new clubs nearly quadrupled, reaching 1 million clubs. Running clubs grew 3.5x and **club-organized events grew 1.5x**, "turning online communities into regular in-person gatherings". **[company]** — [Strava press](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-revealing-that-doomscrolling-is-out-movement-is-in-302631107.html)
- Strava has a **Club Event Insights** tool that counts real-world attendance from GPS-recorded activities. The app "proves" offline attendance automatically, with no manual roll call — [Strava Support: Club Event Insights](https://support.strava.com/hc/en-us/articles/36495511851917-Club-Event-Insights)
- Strava subscriber retention is reported at 80–90%. **[secondary]** — [Runify blog](https://www.runifyapp.com/blog/strava-usage-statistics)
- **Peloton** committed to tripling in-person events in 2025 and growing instructor-led in-person events up to 10x by FY2027. In one quarter, instructors attended more than 40 events, 3x the year before. **[secondary, quoting earnings]** — [Peloton Buddy](https://www.pelobuddy.com/10x-instructor-events/)
- Claims that Peloton members who engage socially work out 15% more, and that tribes/stories lift retention 20%. **[secondary, unverified]** No primary Peloton source found — [researchci](https://researchci.com/the-network-effect-why-pelotons-community-is-its-secret-weapon-amidst-competition)
- Peloton social features: follow, high-five during a workout, leaderboard, and "Sessions" for scheduled group workouts with friends — [Peloton blog: community features](https://www.onepeloton.com/blog/peloton-community-features)

### Inferences
- The model that best matches BK is **Orangetheory**, mapped to BK's in-class tests (ET): the objective metric is generated **in the room**, by the **real performance of each person against their own threshold**, and shown on the **TV during class**. The app is then the **memory and accumulation** of what happened in the room. BK's in-class tests and attendance are its "Splat Points". The app's job is to collect and celebrate them, not to invent a separate economy.
- "Personal threshold" (Orangetheory zones based on each person's heart rate) is how to make the in-class leaderboard less unfair to weaker students: rank by **progress against yourself** or by effort, not only by absolute score.
- Strava shows the reverse loop: a group in the app becomes a real-world event. For BK, the class group or friends group in the app can "commit" to a game night or to an in-class team goal.

### Gaps
- I found no audited retention data for Orangetheory, F45, ClassPass or Peloton that separates people who attend in person from those who don't. F45, ClassPass and Nike Run Club were not researched for lack of budget.

## Q3. Retail and loyalty: Starbucks, Highlands/Phúc Long, Pokémon GO and real-world events

### Takeaway
Starbucks proves at scale that a points economy plus an app, tied to **physical visits**, can account for the majority of revenue. Pokémon GO and its academic research show that **playing with people you know in real life** is the main reason players stay. That is exactly BK's premise ("students play because they know each other"), and it comes with a warning: when the friend group churns, the drop is fast.

### Cited Findings
- **Starbucks Rewards**: more than 35 million 90-day active members, an all-time high. Members drove nearly 60% of US company-operated revenue in FY2025, more than $13B. **[company, SEC filing]** — [Starbucks 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/829224/000082922425000114/sbux-20250928.htm); [8-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/829224/000082922425000013/sbux-12292024xexhibit991.htm)
- In 2026, Starbucks "reimagined" Rewards with new levels (tiers) and exclusive perks. I could not read the full press release (403) — [Starbucks press 2026](https://about.starbucks.com/press/2026/starbucks-reimagines-its-rewards-program-with-new-levels-and-exclusive-perks/)
- Moving to a spend-based program grew Starbucks loyalty membership by 13% (2019) — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/starbucks-boosts-loyalty-membership-by-13-following-switch-to-spend-based/553615/)
- **Highlands Coffee (Vietnam)**: 1 DRIP for every 10,000 VND spent, with Gold, Platinum and Black tiers. The original page now returns 404, so I could not verify the details — [pokemonblog (404 at fetch time)](https://pokemonblog.com/2024/10/25/niantic-announces-new-benefits-for-attending-community-meetups-plus-updates-to-its-community-ambassador-program-with-pokemon-go/)

- The Pokémon GO lesson for BK: the social network inside a real class is a **double-edged retention moat**. Friends keep each other playing, but if a class "goes quiet", the whole group can leave together. Monitor engagement by **class/friend cluster**, not only per individual.

## Q4. School-linked (ClassDojo, parents, teacher praise, public recognition)

### Takeaway
The most solid evidence (RCTs and meta-analyses) is on **sending parents information** (Bergman: texting parents cut course failures by 39%) and **teacher behavior-specific praise**. A class-wide point system (ClassDojo / Good Behavior Game) reduces disruptive behavior, but there is **no evidence it improves learning**. Hill & Tyson show the parent role that helps most is **"academic socialization"** (talking about expectations and the value of learning), while **helping with homework is negatively associated with achievement**.

### Cited Findings
- **Bergman & Chan (RCT, 22 middle and high schools, West Virginia)**: automated texts to parents about missed assignments, grades and absences → **course failures −39%, class attendance +17%**, about +0.25 GPA for students with below-average GPA, and higher retention in the district. **No effect on test scores.** **[RCT]** — [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/sending-parents-useful-information-attendance-course-progress-big-effects-social-scientists-find/); [SSRN paper](https://doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.2989472); [J-PAL brief](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/2017.09.27-texted-hechinger.pdf)
- **Hill & Tyson (2009, meta-analysis of 50 studies, more than 50,000 middle school students)**: parental involvement is positively associated with achievement, **except helping with homework**. **Academic socialization** (communicating expectations, linking school to future goals, discussing learning strategies) has the strongest association, around 0.39, while helping with homework is around −0.11. **[meta]** — [ERIC EJ838541](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ838541); [Harvard: Nancy Hill](https://scholar.harvard.edu/nancyhill/publications/parental-involvement-middle-school-meta-analytic-assessment-strategies)
- **ClassDojo + Good Behavior Game** (including in secondary classrooms): reduces disruptive behavior and increases academically engaged behavior, and teachers find it acceptable. **[single-case studies]** — [ResearchGate: GBG with ClassDojo in secondary classrooms](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361036312_Evaluation_of_the_Good_Behavior_Game_Using_ClassDojo_in_Secondary_Classrooms)
- "Tootling" through ClassDojo, where students report each other's good deeds and the class earns points toward a shared goal, improves class behavior — [School Psychology Review 2019](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.17105/SPR-2017-0090.V48-1)
- Review: ClassDojo's contribution is limited to behavior management, with **no evidence of better learning outcomes**. Competing for points can cause stress — [ResearchGate review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355791131_A_Review_on_the_Contribution_of_ClassDojo_as_Point_System_Gamification_in_Education)
- **Behavior-specific praise**: linked to more on-task behavior and less challenging behavior. O'Handley et al. (2020) found that across 4 **secondary** classrooms, more behavior-specific praise from teachers raised engaged behavior and reduced disruption. A 4:1 praise-to-reprimand ratio is commonly recommended, but research on the exact ratio is thin — [Frontiers in Education 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2024.1441739/pdf); [IRIS Center, Vanderbilt](https://iris.peabody.vanderbilt.edu/module/bi2-elem/cresource/q1/p02/); [ERIC ED606564](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED606564.pdf)

### Inferences
- The highest-evidence parent mechanism for BK is a **short, actionable, automatic alert** (missed homework, absence, in-class test result), not a pretty dashboard. The effect is largest for **weaker students**. Bergman's lesson: it improves behavior (attendance, submissions) more than test scores.
- Following Hill & Tyson, parent reports should prompt parents to **talk about goals and progress** ("ask your child about the 'X' problem type they just mastered"), not to help with homework.
- A class-wide shared goal ("tootling" / Good Behavior Game) is a well-evidenced way to run "class-wide games run by teachers" in a cooperative form, with less pressure than individual rankings.

### Gaps
- No research on Seesaw or on ClassDojo's effect on parent engagement or retention at private centers. No research on "displaying a class goal on the TV" in secondary classrooms specifically.

## Q5. Mechanism list: evidence for each

### Takeaway
Ranked by strength of evidence: (1) parent alerts [RCT]; (2) teacher behavior-specific praise [many studies]; (3) class-wide shared goal / team game [single-case studies]; (4) physical rewards / prize wheel / merch [commercial practice, **no outcome data**, plus a warning from the Deci meta-analysis]; (5) attendance streaks / attendance awards [**an RCT shows a negative effect** if done wrong]; (6) seasonal ceremonies / Year in Review [practice only].

### Cited Findings
- **Check-in reward / prize wheel**: Mathnasium uses prize wheels, Plinko and mystery boxes in-center, with no effect data **[marketing]** — [Mathnasium Paradise Valley](https://www.mathnasium.com/math-centers/paradisevalley/news/rewards-mathnasium-paradise-valley). Niantic gives benefits for checking into meetups (Campfire, 1.9M check-ins in a year) **[company, snippet]** — [Pokémon GO live events](https://ultimatepopculture.fandom.com/wiki/Pok%C3%A9mon_Go_live_events)
- **Attendance awards: RCT warning.** Robinson, Gallus, Lee & Rogers (OBHDP 2019, RCT, N = 15,329): **pre-announced** awards for perfect attendance **had no effect**, and **surprise** awards for past attendance **reduced** later attendance. Mechanism: the award signals that perfect attendance is *not* the norm or expectation, and it "licenses" recipients who feel they have already exceeded it. **[RCT]** — [Harvard PDF](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/todd_rogers/files/the_demotivating_effect_and_unintended_message_of_awards_vf.pdf); [ERIC ED605659](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED605659.pdf); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/retrospective-awards/). Washington State's education department (OSPI) has a "Rethinking Perfect Attendance Awards" document — [OSPI 2024](https://ospi.k12.wa.us/sites/default/files/2024-10/rethinking-perfect-attendance-awards-c-gerlich.pdf)
- **Tangible rewards and intrinsic motivation**: Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999, 128 experiments): expected tangible rewards reduce free-choice intrinsic motivation (engagement-contingent d = −0.40, completion-contingent −0.36, performance-contingent −0.28). **The harm is larger in children than in college students.** **[meta]** — [Deci/Koestner/Ryan 1999 PDF](https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/642/articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf); [Review of Educational Research 2001](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543071001001). **Contested**: Cameron, Eisenberger and colleagues argue the effect is overstated and limited to specific conditions — [PubMed comment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589298); [PubMed: "the myth continues"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22478353/)
- **Leaderboards + badges + coins in a course**: Hanus & Fox (2015, a semester-long longitudinal study) found intrinsic motivation, satisfaction and final exam scores were lower in the gamified class than the control, attributed to social comparison and the focus on rewards **[peer-reviewed]** — [ResearchGate: Hanus & Fox 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265644737_Assessing_the_effects_of_gamification_in_the_classroom_A_longitudinal_study_on_intrinsic_motivation_social_comparison_satisfaction_effort_and_academic_performance)
- **Parent reports**: Bergman RCT (see Q4) — [Hechinger](https://hechingerreport.org/sending-parents-useful-information-attendance-course-progress-big-effects-social-scientists-find/)
- **Teacher kudos / public praise**: behavior-specific praise increases engagement (see Q4) — [IRIS Center](https://iris.peabody.vanderbilt.edu/module/bi2-elem/cresource/q1/p02/)
- **Class-wide goals / team competition**: Good Behavior Game with ClassDojo and tootling (see Q4) — [School Psychology Review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.17105/SPR-2017-0090.V48-1)
- **In-session metric shown on TV, then into the app**: Orangetheory (see Q2) — [Kobie](https://kobie.com/orangetheory-driving-loyalty-through-gamification/)
- **Year in Review / seasonal ceremonies**: Strava Year in Sport, Orangetheory 12 Days of Fitness **[company]** — [Strava press](https://press.strava.com/articles/strava-releases-12th-annual-year-in-sport-trend-report-2025); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241125821512/en/Orangetheory-Fitness-Unveils-12-Days-of-Fitness-A-Data-Driven-Celebration-of-2024s-Most-Popular-Workouts)

### Inferences
- **Attendance streaks are the riskiest mechanism on BK's list**. The Robinson RCT shows that rewarding attendance itself can tell students "attendance is not the norm". Better to treat attendance as the **default norm** ("23/24 students in your class came on time today"), give rewards for **what you do in class** (effort, progress on a problem type), and **never** hand out surprise "you were great at attending" awards after the fact.
- The Deci meta-analysis says **expected tangible rewards** (xu → physical prizes) are the most likely to erode intrinsic motivation, especially for younger children. Grades 6–7 are more at risk than grades 10–12. Mitigations from self-determination theory: (a) reward **unexpectedly** or **informationally** ("you have mastered problem type X"), not as a contract; (b) keep xu as a "fun currency" and don't pay xu for the core learning tasks themselves; (c) put more weight on verbal and social recognition (praise in class, a shout-out on TV).
- The Hanus & Fox lesson: piling many game elements (leaderboard + badges + coins) onto a learning course can **lower** motivation over a semester. BK already has all of these. It should measure motivation over time, not just usage.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous study of "physical prizes / merch / prize wheels" at tutoring centers. No public study on "app progress celebrated in class" or on "seasonal offline ceremonies" in education.

## Q6. Risks: game-show classrooms, unfairness to absent students, parent perception

### Takeaway
There are three risks with evidence behind them: (1) **extrinsic rewards and leaderboards wear down intrinsic motivation** (Deci meta-analysis, Hanus & Fox); (2) **attendance awards send the wrong signal** (Robinson RCT); (3) **competing for points causes stress** (ClassDojo review). "Unfairness to absent students" and "parent perception" have no direct research, but there is solid indirect evidence: parents respond strongly to **actionable information**, not entertainment (Bergman), and the most effective parent role is talking with the child about learning (Hill & Tyson).

### Cited Findings
- Tangible rewards undermine intrinsic motivation, more strongly in children — [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/642/articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf)
- Leaderboards and badges reduce motivation and satisfaction over a semester — [Hanus & Fox 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265644737_Assessing_the_effects_of_gamification_in_the_classroom_A_longitudinal_study_on_intrinsic_motivation_social_comparison_satisfaction_effort_and_academic_performance)
- Competing for positive points can cause stress, and there is no evidence of learning gains from point systems — [ClassDojo review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/355791131_A_Review_on_the_Contribution_of_ClassDojo_as_Point_System_Gamification_in_Education)
- Attendance awards have no effect or a negative one — [Robinson et al. 2019](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/todd_rogers/files/the_demotivating_effect_and_unintended_message_of_awards_vf.pdf)
- A friend-driven community churns quickly once it starts to break up — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-go-with-friends/)
- Parent information about missed assignments, grades and absences cuts failures and raises attendance, with the biggest effect for weaker students — [Bergman & Chan](https://hechingerreport.org/sending-parents-useful-information-attendance-course-progress-big-effects-social-scientists-find/)

### Inferences (patterns BK can exploit, and guardrails)
1. **"The room makes the data, the app keeps the memory"** (Orangetheory pattern). In-class tests, attendance and teacher feedback are the "Splat Points". The app celebrates and accumulates them. Avoid a separate app economy that doesn't depend on the classroom, because then the app competes with the classroom instead of reinforcing it.
2. **Rank against your own threshold** (Orangetheory zones). In-class leaderboards and TV screens should rank by **progress against yourself** or by **class-wide team goals** (Good Behavior Game / tootling), not only by absolute score. That reduces stress (ClassDojo review) and social comparison (Hanus & Fox).
3. **Attendance = a norm, not a prize** (Robinson RCT). Drop "perfect attendance" awards and after-the-fact attendance streak rewards. Use social-norm messages instead ("your class is at 96% attendance"). The check-in wheel should be **a small, random ritual of arriving** (fun, low value), not a "reward for not being absent".
4. **For absent students**: offer a **makeup path through the app** (the absent student finishes the session's practice in the app and earns the same xu for that part), so the game layer doesn't punish them twice. This fits BK's existing makeup-session flow. *(An inference; there is no direct research.)*
5. **Parent channel = short, actionable alerts plus "talk about learning" prompts** (Bergman + Hill & Tyson). Automatic alerts for absences, missed homework and in-class test results. Monthly reports that prompt parents to ask their child about the problem types they have mastered, rather than helping with homework. This is the mechanism with the strongest evidence on the list.
6. **Xu = fun currency, not a learning contract** (Deci). Don't pay xu for the core learning work (homework, in-class tests). Reward unexpectedly and informationally. Physical prizes are tied to **the ritual and the community** (game nights, ceremonies), not to each piece of homework. Watch grades 6–7 most closely.
7. **Monitor engagement by friend cluster or class** (Pokémon GO). A whole class going quiet is an early churn signal for the group.
8. **Seasonal ceremony + "Year in Review"** (Strava, Orangetheory). Turn a semester of in-class tests and attendance into a recap that is shown at an in-person event and can be shared, so offline data becomes social content.
9. **Guarding against the "game show" feel**: keep class-wide games short and tied to the session's problem types, using a team (tootling) format. Measure motivation and learning over time, not just usage (the Hanus & Fox lesson).

### Gaps
- No direct research on Vietnamese parents' perceptions of gamification or rewards at tutoring centers. No data on the effect of on-site game nights on re-enrollment.
